# Validity links beat strength and confidence scores

The flat memory store is good enough for the core job. Add validity and provenance before you add strength or a numeric confidence. No published agent-memory system isolates the gain of a per-memory strength or importance score. No system in the notes publishes a measured, calibrated per-memory confidence. No memory benchmark scores calibration. The failures that benchmarks do measure are about stale and conflicting facts. Systems mostly **omit** updates (52-98% omission on HaluMem), and they almost never invent them (below 1.2%) ([HaluMem](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03506v2)). LLM readers follow stale notes (stale-value reliance 0.92-1.00). Conflict pre-resolution before the model reads the notes gives near-ceiling accuracy ([Memory Trust Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01852)). A use-based ACT-R strength has real evidence, but only outside agent memory. In hashtag reuse it beat recency-only by about 3-5% and frequency-only by about 9-42% ([Kowald et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1701.01276)). A calibrated [0,1] confidence needs labelled feedback that the store does not collect yet. Platt scaling needs a calibration set, and isotonic regression needs about 1,000 labels ([Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~alexn/papers/calibration.icml05.crc.rev3.pdf)). Thus the smallest design that the evidence supports has these parts: bi-temporal validity on supersede, a separate "contradicts" link that shows both sides, provenance for each memory, and evidence counters. A use log can feed an optional, bounded ACT-R rerank. Turn the rerank on only after an offline harness shows a gain.

## No agent-memory system has measured the gain of a per-memory score

Only three widely cited systems keep an explicit per-memory number. Generative Agents uses an LLM importance of 1-10 plus recency decay. MemoryBank uses an Ebbinghaus strength S. MemoryOS uses a segment "heat". **None of the three publishes an ablation that removes the score alone** ([Generative Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2), [MemoryBank](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10250), [MemoryOS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06326)). The Generative Agents ablation removes whole modules (observation, reflection, planning). It does not remove the importance or recency term. Thus the large believability gap (TrueSkill 29.89 full versus 21.21 with no memory, Cohen's d = 8.16) does not measure the score formula ([Generative Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)). Mem0, Zep/Graphiti, A-MEM, HippoRAG and Memory-R1 keep no per-memory strength or confidence field ([Mem0](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413), [Zep](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956), [A-MEM](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12110)).

The best-measured systems store more and process less. On LoCoMo in Mem0's own harness, **full context (J 72.90) beat every memory system**. The flat Mem0 store (J 66.88, p95 search 0.200 s, 1,764 context tokens) came within 1.6 points of its graph variant Mem0g (68.44) ([Mem0](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)). In "Useful Memories Become Faulty When Continuously Updated by LLMs", utility first rises and then falls as LLM consolidation continues. It can drop below no memory. GPT-5.4 fails 54% of ARC-AGI problems that it solved without memory. In ARC-AGI Stream, agents that keep raw episodes **double the accuracy** of agents forced to consolidate ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12978)). MemPalace stores verbatim text with no strength or confidence. It reports 96.6% LongMemEval R@5 with raw semantic search and no LLM call. This is an author claim of retrieval recall, not QA accuracy. The authors also say that the last 0.6% toward 100% came from "teaching to the test" ([MemPalace](https://github.com/mempalace/mempalace)). These results support the rule "compaction never merges similar text".

## ACT-R activation has real evidence, but not in agent memory

The ACT-R base-level learning equation is the simplest formula with direct evidence that it ranks "needed now" items better than recency or frequency alone. The exact form is A_i(t) = ln( Σ_{j=1..n} (t − t_j)^(−d) ). Every past use adds to activation, and an older use adds less. The decay d "quickly became a parameter that was treated as a constant (d=.5)" ([van Rijn et al. 2009](https://www.ai.rug.nl/~leendert/pubs/VanRijn-ICCM2009.pdf)). The environment supports this shape. In New York Times headlines, child-directed speech and one person's e-mail, the probability that an item is needed next day is linear in its past frequency (R² = .964 to .999) ([Anderson & Schooler 1991](https://dyurovsky.github.io/learning-humans-machines/class/13-class/papers/anderson1991.pdf)). The authors still write: "we have no investment in the claim that these empirical functions are best modeled or correctly modeled as power functions". ACT-R uses an approximation for long histories. A hybrid mode keeps the last k uses exact and approximates older uses ([Bothell, ACT-R list](https://mailman.srv.cs.cmu.edu/pipermail/act-r-users/2006-February/001103.html)). The hybrid is "more robust to violations of the assumption of equally spaced retrievals" (search snippet, paywalled paper) ([Fisher et al. 2018](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2Fs42113-018-0015-3.pdf)).

The measured gain is small over recency and large over raw counts. In Twitter hashtag reuse, individual BLL gave MRR@10 .193 vs .188 (recency) vs .136 (frequency) on CompSci. On Random it gave .314 vs .300 vs .261. **The gap over recency is not tested for significance** ([Kowald et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1701.01276)). The fitted decay was d = 1.7, "much higher than the suggested value" of 0.5. Bursty data wants faster decay. In the flashcard benchmark, plain ACT-R has log loss 0.4033 but AUC 0.5225, close to random. FSRS-6 reaches 0.3460 and 0.7034 ([srs-benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)). The scheduler sets the review times there, so that AUC does not transfer to an unscheduled store.

How strength must combine with relevance has no measured answer. No source measured how often a strength term hides a relevant but rarely used memory. No source gives a tuned weight. The notes also found no study that tests BLL against recency on note, file or e-mail re-access logs. They found no RRF test of a strength list against plain keyword plus vector RRF. Thus a strength term in this store is an untested hypothesis. It is cheap, but it is not a known gain.

## A calibrated confidence needs labels that the store does not collect

The smallest defensible confidence model is a Beta-Bernoulli evidence counter. In subjective logic, an opinion ω = (b, d, u, a) has b + d + u = 1 and projected probability P = b + a·u. In evidence form, α = r + aW and β = s + (1−a)W, and u = W/(r+s+W) ([Subjective logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subjective_logic)). The posterior mean is E[p] = (r + aW)/(r + s + W). With a = 0.5 and W = 2 this becomes (r+1)/(r+s+2). The Beta Reputation System adds two parts. "feedback from highly reputed agents should carry more weight", and a forgetting factor λ is "adjusted according to the expected rapidity of change in the observed entity" ([Jøsang & Ismail 2002](https://mn.uio.no/ifi/english/people/aca/josang/publications/ji2002-bled.pdf)). TruthFinder sets source trust to the average confidence of the facts that the source gives, and iterates ([TruthFinder copy](https://www.scribd.com/doc/55462205/kdd07-xyin)). That method depends on independent sources.

This model is easy to compute, but it is not calibrated. Calibration needs ground-truth labels. Knowledge Vault shows the full recipe at scale. It calibrated fused scores with Platt scaling on a validation set: "If we collect all the triples that have a predicted probability of 0.9, then we find that about 90% of them are indeed true" ([Dong et al. 2014](https://lunadong.com/publication/kv_kdd.pdf)). Platt scaling beats isotonic regression below about 200-1,000 labels. Isotonic is as good or better at 1,000 labels or more. For a model that is already well calibrated, calibration "actually hurts performance when the calibration sets are small" ([Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~alexn/papers/calibration.icml05.crc.rev3.pdf)). Temperature scaling cut ResNet-110 CIFAR-100 ECE from 16.53% to 1.26%. On the 20 News NLP task, histogram binning (3.6%) beat temperature (4.11%) ([Guo et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.04599)). A memory store that has no "this memory was right / wrong" feedback events has nothing to fit.

LLM-stated confidence is a weak input. Verbalized confidence values "predominantly fall within the 80% to 100% range". Vanilla ECE exceeds 0.377 for GPT-3, GPT-3.5 and Vicuna, and GPT-4 AUROC is "merely 62.7%" ([Xiong et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)). GPT-4 on GSM8K reaches ECE 0.064 because it gives every sample 100%. **A low ECE can hide zero discrimination**, so always report AUROC next to ECE. Sampling 5 answers and checking agreement raised GSM8K AUROC from 54.8% to 92.7% ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)). RLHF models' verbalized confidence "often" cuts ECE "by a relative 50%" against token probabilities ([Tian et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975)). That is an improvement on a bad base, not a calibrated output. Kadavath et al. report good P(True) calibration "in the right format", but P(IK) "struggle[s]" on new tasks (abstract only, no numbers) ([Kadavath et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)).

The low/medium/high enum is therefore not the weak point. A [0,1] number that nobody can calibrate gives false precision. An enum set by the writer states the same information honestly. The evidence counters (r, s) and the uncertainty u can later produce a [0,1] score, after feedback labels exist.

## Stale facts, not uncertain facts, cause the measured failures

The 2026 evidence points at validity, not at confidence. In "The Memory Trust Gap", stale-value reliance is 0.92-1.00 across Qwen3 0.6B-8B. Larger models resist a plain stale note, but they are harmed when the stale note *appears newer*. Metadata improves accuracy at all sizes, and conflict pre-resolution gives near-ceiling accuracy ([Hu & Ramachandran 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01852)). ClashEval shows the same reader credulity. LLMs adopt incorrect retrieved content over a correct prior "over 60% of the time" ([ClashEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198)). MemConflict finds that answer correctness often diverges from retrieval quality. Performance falls with longer histories and larger conflict distance (abstract only) ([MemConflict](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20926)). TANGLE tests whether agents keep unresolvable conflicting evidence instead of forcing one answer (snippet only) ([TANGLE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13921)).

"Prefer newer" works for mutable facts, but not always. Zep's newer-wins invalidation raised LongMemEval knowledge-update accuracy with gpt-4o (78.2% → 83.3%). It lowered accuracy with gpt-4o-mini (76.9% → 74.4%) ([Zep](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956)). The notes found no controlled test of "newer wins" against "evidence wins". AGM belief revision gives the principle: "give up beliefs only when forced to do so, and ... give up as few of them as possible". Its "success" postulate is the most debated one, because an agent can refuse an input ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)). Reason maintenance (JTMS, ATMS) records why each belief is held, so the system can retract a belief when its support fails ([Reason maintenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason_maintenance)). TOKI treats contradiction resolution as write-time concurrency control. It keeps the losing fact in an audit row, and its authors call the cross-system comparison underpowered ([TOKI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06240)).

Cheap contradiction detection is possible, but generic NLI transfers poorly. cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base scores 92.38 on SNLI and 90.04 on MNLI-mismatched ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base)). On dialogue contradictions (DECODE), RoBERTa trained on SNLI+MNLI scores **77.40%**. The same model trained on DECODE scores 93.19% ([Nie et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.13391)). The structured pairwise method "is more robust and transferable" than one that reads the whole history. LLM judges have high precision and low recall on conflict detection. Claude-3 Sonnet with CoT reached precision 0.951 and recall 0.566. Llama-3.1-8B reached recall 0.010. Pair contradictions are much easier (0.89) than self-contradictions (best 0.454) ([arXiv 2504.00180](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00180v1)). SUMMEDITS finds most LLMs near random at factual inconsistency (search summary only) ([Laban et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14540)). On CPU, DeBERTa-v3-large zero-shot NLI took a 728 ms median per request on 16 threads, against 36.1 ms on GPU (search summary only) ([arXiv 2610.30970](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30970)). The notes have no first-party CPU latency for the xsmall, small or base models. Small int8 ONNX exports exist ([HF nli-deberta-v3-small](https://huggingface.co/cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-small/tree/main/onnx)). Measure them locally.

Poisoned writes are a larger documented risk than score math. AgentPoison reports 82% retrieval success and 63% end-to-end attack success at a poisoning ratio below 0.1% ([AgentPoison](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12784v1)). Thus "confirmations" must come from independent sources. If retrieval counts as confirmation, a poisoned or wrong memory gains confidence each time that it ranks high. The notes found no study that measures this loop in LLM memory, so the guard is a design rule, not a finding.

## Benchmarks score updates and conflicts, but none scores calibration

Five benchmarks test outdated or conflicting facts directly. Multi-hop conflict scores are very low everywhere. Single-hop update scores sit near 75-83%. **No benchmark in the notes scores the calibration of a memory store's confidence.** Retrieval against gold evidence IDs avoids judge variance, so make it the main metric.

Two results matter most for the design. First, plain BM25 (48% FC-SH) beat every embedding RAG and most memory products on single-hop conflict ([MemoryAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05257v3)). Hybrid full-text plus vector search is a sound base. Second, a deterministic last-write rule solves most of FactConsolidation ([arXiv 2610.09193](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09193)). A correct supersede path is worth more than any score on these sets.

## The smallest evidence-backed design adds validity, provenance and a use log

Keep the flat store, the hybrid RRF search, the ADD-only writes and compaction that never merges. The current evidence supports all four. Raw episodes beat forced consolidation ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12978)). A flat store is within 1.6 J points of a graph ([Mem0](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)). The newest vendor design moved to ADD-only writes ([Mem0 research](https://mem0.ai/research-3), vendor claim). Then add the parts below in order of evidence. Each part is additive: new columns, a new link type, or a query-time step. None needs an LLM call at query time.

## What the evidence does not support

The evidence does not show that a per-memory strength score improves an LLM agent's answers. Every measured strength gain comes from hashtags, flashcards or word pairs. Every agent-memory ablation removes whole modules, not the score. The evidence does not show that a numeric [0,1] confidence improves answers, or that any memory system has calibrated one. No memory benchmark measures calibration. The evidence does not support the use of LLM-stated confidence as the stored score, because it clusters at 80-100% and discriminates near chance ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)). It does not support "prefer newer" for every kind of fact, because Zep's result differs by reader model. It does not support strength-based deletion. FadeMem's retention gain comes from its own synthetic benchmark ([FadeMem](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18642v2)). It does not support FSRS-style scheduling, because the store has no graded recall outcome. It does not support the claim that MemPalace-style spatial structure beats scoring, because its numbers are tuned retrieval recall. Finally, the notes hold no measurement of rich-get-richer loops in memory scores, no tuned weight between strength and relevance, and no head-to-head test of "flat", "flat plus strength" and "graph" on knowledge updates.

## Conclusion

The question "strength and confidence or not" is the wrong first question. The measured failures of agent memory are omitted updates, stale notes that readers trust, and conflicts that no system resolves across hops. A flat store fixes these failures most cheaply when it records *when a fact was true*, *what replaced or disputes it*, and *where it came from*. It must also resolve those links before the LLM reads the results. Strength and calibrated confidence are good second steps, but only when they are measured. The use log and the evidence counters cost almost nothing to record now. They become a tested ACT-R rerank and a Platt-calibrated confidence after the harness and the feedback labels exist. If you build the scores before the labels, you get numbers that look precise, that nobody can check, and that a feedback loop or a poisoned write can push up.
